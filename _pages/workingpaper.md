### Working papers   

- **Luoye Chen and Madhu Khanna, “Weather Shocks and Economic Triggers of Cropland Changein the US: A Fine-scale Spatial Analysis”.** *(Job Market Paper, lead author)*

[Abstract] Land conversions between cultivated cropland and grassland are a pressing concern because of their substantial implications for food supply and ecosystem services. Despite the importance, there has been extremely limited understanding of the frequency and determinants of crop-grass conversions. Using high-resolution data on 500-meter land parcels in the rainfed United States from 2001 to 2017, we provide the first largescale investigation of how US farmers and landowners adjust crop-grass conversions in response to weather shocks and commodity price fluctuations. We also characterize heterogeneity in these adjustments across land parcels with different biophysical attributes using generic machine learning inference. We find that the contemporary rainfall shocks, long-term climate change, and lagged received commodity prices affect the likelihood of crop-grass conversions. The effects of rainfall shocks and price fluctuations on crop-grass conversions are mainly confined to land parcels at the margin of agricultural production, with relatively lower water storage capacity, less productivity, and steeper slopes. These findings suggest that crop-grass conversion is not a static phenomenon and that it is important to examine how it evolves in response to various factors that may change over time.

- **Luoye Chen and Madhu Khanna, “Heterogeneous Effects of a Changing Climate on Bird Biodi-versity and Adaptability”.** *(Submitted, lead author)*

[Abstract] There is considerable concern about the alarming decline in bird biodiversity and limited systematic understanding of the magnitude of the effect of a changing climate on bird biodiversity and the extent to which birds can adapt to these changes across species and regions. Using a long-term dataset of the North American bird population from 1980 to 2015, we find statistically robust evidence that swapping one standard deviation of days in the 0 to 15 °C range for corresponding days above 25 °C decreases bird abundance and species richness by 1.7%~2.5%; these effects are larger for specialist birds (2.8%~4.9%), and for long-distance migrant specialist species (2.9%~5.2%), and bird population in the drier areas such as the West (2.5%~7.0%). We do not find statistically significant evidence of the ability for US bird population to adapt and offset the negative impact of climate change in the long run. Under the RCP 8.5 climate predictions, we project a 16% and 9% decline in the abundance and species richness of specialist birds, respectively, by the end of this century. The corresponding effects on generalist birds are smaller but also significant.



 - **Xiaoguang Chen, Luoye Chen, Wei Xie, Nathan Mueller, Steven Davis, “Air Pollution and FlightDelays in China".** *Revise and Resubmit, Journal of Environmental Economics and Management, (first authorship is jointly shared by Xiaoguang Chen, Luoye Chen, and Wei Xie)*

[Abstract] Flight delays adversely affect millions of passengers worldwide every year, with substantial 38 economic costs. Although a long list of factors are known to contribute to such delays, the 39 effect of air pollution on flight delays has never been assessed. Here, we analyze how the 40 concentration of PM2.5 affects flight departure delays using daily data from 131 airports in 41 China, the world’s largest aviation market. We find that, on average, a one-standard-42 deviation increase in the daily PM2.5 concentration leads to roughly 13 additional minutes 43 in gross departure delay per flight and 3 additional minutes in net departure delay per 44 flight after excluding arrival delays from previous assignments. These effects are more 45 pronounced for larger airline companies and for larger airports. In turn, decreases in 46 PM2.5 concentrations attributable to the Air Pollution Prevention and Control Action Plan 47 in 2013 accounted for 15-33% of the total reduction in national average net flight delays in 48 2016 and 2017. In total, we estimate reductions in China’s PM2.5 pollution from 2016 to 49 2017 avoided 2.4-3.8 billion US$ in passenger time and labor losses, offsetting 50 approximately 5-8% of China’s average annual costs of PM2.5 control measures 2013-2017. 51 Our results highlight a previously neglected impact of air pollution and further support 52 China’s ambitious clean air policies.
